01 · The desk

Eleven skills. One trading desk. $0.

The pack is called trader-skill, built by Jake Schincariol (@opusjake) and released under the MIT licence. It's plain Python reading free public data (SEC EDGAR, the House Clerk, news feeds), so there's no API key, no signup and nothing to pay. It never trades on its own: every order needs your yes, and paper trading is the default.

  1. Copycat/trader-copycat

    Rebuilds any fund's portfolio from its SEC 13F: every stock, its weight, and what they bought, added, trimmed and sold since last quarter.

  2. Pelosi tracker/trader-pelosi

    Reads the stock trades members of Congress disclose and alerts you once per new filing.

  3. News monitor/trader-news

    Headlines and SEC 8-K filings for every stock you hold or watch.

  4. Stock screener/trader-screener

    Screens companies against your own rules and ranks what passes.

  5. Earnings analyst/trader-analyst

    Revenue, profit, EPS and margins straight from the company's SEC filings.

  6. Budget manager/trader-budget

    Sizes every position to your budget, keeps a cash buffer, caps any one stock.

  7. Risk manager/trader-risk

    Checks every order against your limits and blocks anything over them.

  8. Order desk/trader-broker

    Turns an approved order into a ticket for your broker. Paper by default, never without your yes.

  9. Price alerts/trader-alerts

    Rings at your price levels, your stop, and the opening bell.

  10. Rebalancer/trader-rebalance

    Brings your portfolio back to its target weights when it drifts.

  11. Tax helper/trader-tax

    Tracks cost basis, gains and losses, and wash-sale windows.

02 · Setup

Install it in two minutes.

These skills run inside Claude Code, Anthropic's coding assistant. Already have it? Jump to step 2.

  • 1

    Get Claude Code, in either form. You need a paid Claude plan (Pro or Max, or an Anthropic API account). The free plan doesn't include Claude Code.

  1. Option A · Claude Desktop (easiest)

    No terminal needed. Download the Claude desktop app for Mac or Windows, sign in, and click the Code tab at the top of the sidebar. The app itself is free, but the Code tab needs a Pro or Max plan. Then go straight to step 2.

  2. Option B · Claude Code in the terminal

    Open a terminal (Mac: the Terminal app; Windows: PowerShell) and paste the line for your computer, below.

Mac · Linux
curl -fsSL https://claude.ai/install.sh | bash
Windows · PowerShell
irm https://claude.ai/install.ps1 | iex

Give the installer a minute (it shows no progress bar). Then open a new terminal window, type claude and press Enter, and log in with your Claude account in the browser window that opens. Official guide: code.claude.com/docs/en/setup.

Also needed: Python 3.8 or newer. Macs usually have it. On Windows, install it from python.org and tick "Add python.exe to PATH". Or just ask Claude Code: "Check I have Python 3.8+ and help me install it if not." Nothing else to install: the skills use only Python's built-in library.
  • 2

    Install the skills. In Claude Desktop's Code tab or Claude Code, paste this in and send it:

Paste into Claude
https://github.com/Jakeschincariol/trader-skill

install skill

Or install it yourself in Claude Code, as a plugin (commands get a /trader-skill: prefix) or by copying the folders (short names like /trader-copycat):

Claude Code · plugin
/plugin marketplace add Jakeschincariol/trader-skill
/plugin install trader-skill@trader-skill
Terminal · Mac / Linux
git clone https://github.com/Jakeschincariol/trader-skill.git
cp -r trader-skill/trader-* ~/.claude/skills/
  • 3

    Tell the SEC who's asking. The SEC asks every tool that reads its filings to identify itself (name + email, used only in the request header). Tell Claude: "Set my SEC user agent to Your Name you@email.com". That's it. You're set up.

03 · Copycat

Name any investor. See their whole portfolio.

Every fund managing over $100M has to file a 13F with the SEC each quarter, listing the US stocks it owns. Copycat finds the fund, downloads the filing, matches every row to a ticker, and compares it with the quarter before. Try these, in plain English:

  • 1

    Warren Buffett. "Show me Berkshire Hathaway's latest 13F. What did they buy and sell?"

  • 2

    Ray Dalio's fund. "What did Bridgewater dump last quarter?"

  • 3

    The biggest of them all. "BlackRock's top 10 holdings."

  • 4

    Copy it to your budget. "Copy Berkshire's top 5 with $5,000, keep 10% cash." Copycat writes the weights; the budget skill turns them into share counts. Nothing is bought.

04 · Congress

The moment Pelosi files a trade, you know.

Under the STOCK Act, members of Congress must disclose stock trades over $1,000 within 45 days. The filings land on the House Clerk's website as PDFs that almost nobody reads. The Pelosi skill reads them, turns each one into clean rows (ticker, buy or sell, date, amount range) and alerts you once per new filing. It works for any House member, not just her.

  • 1

    See her trades this year. "Show me Nancy Pelosi's stock disclosures this year."

  • 2

    Get pinged. "Check for new Pelosi filings every morning and tell me when one posts." In Claude Code you can put it on a schedule.

  • 3

    Check any member. "Any new trades from members of Congress this week?"

Know the delay. You see a trade when it's filed, which can be up to 45 days after it happened. Her famous Nvidia trades made headlines because the stock kept running after she disclosed them. That doesn't happen every time. The amounts come as ranges (like $1M–$5M), exactly as filed.
05 · The proof

Bridgewater sold CoreWeave. Retail held.

Bridgewater, the fund Ray Dalio founded, held 364,025 CoreWeave shares at the end of March. By 30 June it held zero. That went public in its Q2 13F, due by 14 August. Here's what CoreWeave did next:

$55

And the opening chart in the reel: IREN, a retail favourite, closed at $76.41 on 5 Nov 2025. It's $23.95 now, down 69%. Buying the top because the comments section is excited is how most people lose money. Checking what the big funds are doing is a free second opinion.

The honest version: a 13F shows what a fund held on the last day of the quarter, up to 45 days later. You never see a sale the day it happens, and funds sell for many reasons (risk limits, client withdrawals, rebalancing). Bridgewater also trimmed Nvidia and Microsoft, which have done fine. Use it as a signal to check, not a signal to copy blindly. Ray Dalio founded Bridgewater but has since handed over control.

07 · Is it safe?

What I checked before installing.

Read the code. Plain Python using only the built-in library, so no hidden packages get installed.
Only public data sites. SEC EDGAR, OpenFIGI (ticker matching), Yahoo Finance, Stooq, NYSE and the House Clerk. Nothing else.
Can't trade by itself. Orders need your typed yes, then Claude asks again. Paper trading is the default.
MIT licensed, with an offline test suite built from real filings.
It's brand new (released 14 Oct 2026), so expect rough edges. Only install it from github.com/Jakeschincariol/trader-skill, never from copies or look-alike repos.
08 · Before you trade

Know what you're getting.

  1. 13F data is old and partial

    Up to 45 days late, US-listed longs only: no shorts, no cash, no foreign shares. You see what they held, not what they hold today.

  2. Copying a fund ≠ copying its results

    You buy later, at a different price, without their reasons or their hedges.

  3. Free prices can lag

    Yahoo's data is unofficial and delayed. Check anything that matters against your broker.

  4. Start on paper

    Run it for a few weeks with fake money before a real dollar goes in.
