Every game from the reel. Jane Street's own trading card game, plus four free games that drill exactly what quant interviews test. For each one: what it trains, a practice plan and a copy-paste AI coach prompt.

How to use this page

  1. Play one game a day for 10–15 minutes. Short and daily beats one long session.
  2. Follow the practice plan under each game. It tells you what to focus on, so you're training, not just clicking.
  3. Stuck? Use the AI coach. Copy the prompt into ChatGPT or Claude and it will drill you on the same skill and explain what you got wrong.
  4. Write down your score each day. Watching it climb is the whole point.

New to all of this? Go in this order: 02 Mental math → 05 Expected value → 04 Estimation → 03 Bet sizing → 01 Figgie. Figgie is the most fun, but it gets much easier once the other four skills click. These are games with play money only. Nothing here involves real money.

01 · Made by Jane Street

Figgie: the trading card game

Jane Street invented Figgie in 2013 to simulate open-outcry commodities trading, and still teaches it. Four or five players start with play money and a hand from a 40-card deck. One suit is the goal suit, and nobody knows which. For 4 minutes everyone bids and offers on cards. At the bell, each goal-suit card pays $10 from the pot, and whoever holds the most goal cards wins the rest.

Practice plan · 3 sessions of 20 minutes

Your project

Learn to work out the goal suit from what other players buy and sell, then price cards by how sure you are.

  1. Session 1: read the rules page, then play 3 rounds against bots. Just learn the buttons.
  2. Session 2: before trading, count your own hand. Remember the goal suit is the same colour as the 12-card suit, so the suit you see most of is a clue.
  3. Session 3: watch which suit everyone keeps bidding on. That's the order flow telling you what they think. Buy that suit early while it's cheap and sell the others.
  4. After each session, paste your notes into the AI coach below.
Copy into ChatGPT or Claude
I'm learning Figgie, Jane Street's trading card game. Rules: 40 cards, suits of 12, 10, 10 and 8 cards. The goal suit is the same colour as the 12-card suit and has 8 or 10 cards. Each goal card pays $10 at the end, and whoever holds the most goal cards wins the rest of the $200 pot.

Act as my trading coach:
1. My starting hand was: [e.g. 4 spades, 2 clubs, 3 hearts, 1 diamond]. Which suit is most likely the goal suit, and roughly how likely? Show the reasoning simply.
2. Based on that, what's a fair price for one card of each suit?
3. During the round, other players kept bidding on: [suit]. How should that change my guess?

Explain it like I'm new to probability, then give me 3 quick practice hands to work out on my own.

You're done when: you can guess the goal suit before the bell in most rounds, and you finish a session up on chips against bots.

02 · Skill

Mental math: Speed Engine

Trading firms test fast arithmetic in their first rounds because traders do it all day. Speed Engine gives you 60-second rounds. Practice mode has 5 lives and adds 5 seconds for every correct answer. Competitive mode is sudden death for the leaderboard.

PlaySpeed EngineBy MyntBit, a quant interview prep site. Start in Practice mode.Play free ↗
Practice plan · 10 minutes a day for a week

Your project

Double your Speed Engine score in 7 days.

  1. Day 1: play 3 rounds in Practice mode and write down your best score.
  2. Every day after: 5 minutes of drills with the AI coach, then 3 rounds of the game.
  3. Learn the shortcuts the coach shows you, like multiplying by 25 or squaring numbers near 50.
  4. Day 7: play 3 rounds and compare with day 1.
Copy into ChatGPT or Claude
Be my mental math coach for quant trading interviews.

Give me 10 questions at a time, one by one: multiplication of 2-digit numbers, percentages, fractions to decimals, and simple division. Wait for my answer before showing the next one. Don't show answers early.

After each set of 10, tell me my score, which types I was slowest or wrong on, and teach me one shortcut for that type (for example, multiplying by 25, squaring numbers near 50, or turning a fraction into a percentage fast). Then make the next set slightly harder.

You're done when: your day-7 score is about double your day-1 score.

03 · Skill

Bet sizing: Risk Lab

Knowing a bet is good isn't enough. You also have to know how much to put on it. The Kelly criterion gives the size that grows your money fastest without blowing up. Risk Lab makes you size bets round after round. Practice mode gives you 3 saves if you go bust, and competitive mode is 25 rounds with no safety net.

PlayRisk LabBy MyntBit. Kelly criterion bet sizing game.Play free ↗
Practice plan · about 30 minutes

Your project

Learn the Kelly formula well enough to size a bet in your head, then prove it in the game.

  1. Paste the prompt below into ChatGPT or Claude and work through its examples.
  2. Play Risk Lab in Practice mode and size every bet with the formula.
  3. Then try one game betting double the Kelly size and watch what happens to your bankroll.
Copy into ChatGPT or Claude
Teach me the Kelly criterion like I'm a beginner.

1. Explain the formula for a simple bet with win probability p and payout odds b, in plain English.
2. Work through 3 examples, like a coin that lands heads 60% of the time paying even money.
3. Explain why betting MORE than Kelly is worse than betting less, and what "half Kelly" means.
4. Then quiz me with 5 bets, one at a time, and I'll tell you what fraction of my bankroll I'd bet.

This is for learning and a play-money game only. Don't give any real trading or betting advice.

You're done when: you can size a bet in under 10 seconds and finish a Practice game without using a save.

04 · Skill

Estimation: Market Maker

A classic trading interview question: "Make me a market on how many piano tuners there are in New York." You can't know the answer, so you estimate it and quote a range. Market Maker asks order-of-magnitude questions and has you set a bid and ask around your guess.

PlayMarket MakerBy MyntBit. Fermi estimation, quoted as a bid/ask spread.Play free ↗
Practice plan · 15 minutes a day for 5 days

Your project

Get your estimates within 10x of the real answer and learn to set a range you'd actually trade on.

  1. Do 3 estimation questions with the AI coach. Say your reasoning out loud, step by step.
  2. Play Market Maker and keep your spread tight only when you're confident.
  3. Note which kinds of questions you're furthest off on, and practise those with the coach.
Copy into ChatGPT or Claude
Act as a trading interviewer giving me Fermi estimation questions (like "How many golf balls fit in a school bus?").

For each question:
1. Ask it, then wait for my estimate and my reasoning.
2. Ask me to "make a market": a bid and an ask around my estimate.
3. Show me a clean step-by-step estimate and the rough real answer.
4. Tell me whether my range was too wide, too narrow or about right, and which assumption hurt me most.

Give me 5 questions, starting easy and getting harder.

You're done when: most of your estimates land within 10x of the real answer and you can explain every step.

05 · Skill

Expected value: Probability Poker

Expected value is the average result of a bet if you played it many times. Every trade is an EV question. Probability Poker gives you timed EV problems. Practice mode has 5 lives and 30 seconds a question, and competitive mode is sudden death with 15 seconds.

PlayProbability PokerBy MyntBit. Timed expected-value questions.Play free ↗
Practice plan · about 1 hour over a week

Your project

Answer EV questions inside the 30-second limit and explain why each answer is right.

  1. Work through the AI coach's dice and card problems first.
  2. Play Probability Poker in Practice mode. Write down any question you missed.
  3. Paste the missed questions back into the coach and ask for the shortcut.
Copy into ChatGPT or Claude
Teach me expected value for quant trading interviews. I'm a beginner.

1. Explain expected value in plain English with one dice example.
2. Then give me 8 questions one at a time, getting harder: dice games, card draws, coin-flip bets, and "would you take this bet?" questions.
3. Wait for my answer each time. If I'm wrong, show the fastest way to work it out.
4. At the end, list the 3 patterns that come up most in interviews.

You're done when: you get 7 out of 8 coach questions right and clear 10 questions in a Practice game.
