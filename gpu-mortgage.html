$500B · Six giants · One collateral

They just turned AI chips
into mortgages.

Nvidia signed with six of the biggest names in finance — Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR — to mobilize over $500 billion of outside capital for AI infrastructure. The pitch: GPU compute is a long-lived, revenue-generating asset you can lend against, like real estate or toll roads. The detail that makes it real: Nvidia will backstop up to 25% of qualifying loans by supporting the resale value of its own chips. The world's biggest asset manager compared it to the birth of mortgage-backed securities. This is the map of who gets paid — and who's holding the risk.

02

Who gets paid — in order of certainty

TIER 1The arrangers — paid no matter what

Apollo, Blackstone, KKR, Goldman, BlackRock and Brookfield just gained a brand-new, multi-hundred-billion-dollar asset class to originate, structure, securitize and manage — with the manufacturer absorbing a slice of the downside. Fee income is indifferent to whether the collateral performs. The most durable lesson of the mortgage era: the arrangers won even when the houses didn't.

→ APO · BX · KKR · GS · BLK · BN — buy the casino, not the chips

TIER 2Nvidia — a double-edged win

Demand stops being capped by customers' balance sheets: every neocloud, enterprise and sovereign that couldn't pay cash can now borrow. And the residual backstop props up the used-GPU market, which protects new-chip pricing. The other edge: this is vendor financing with extra steps — a contingent liability that grows with every loan written. The last time a dominant vendor financed its own customers at scale was telecom equipment in 1999. Ask those shareholders how the decade went.

→ NVDA — thesis intact, risk profile changed. Track the contingencies in the filings.

TIER 3The levered compute layer

GPU-collateralized borrowing already existed — CoreWeave pioneered it. This institutionalizes and cheapens it: deeper debt against fleets means faster growth and cheaper refinancing for the neoclouds. The mirror image: they're the most exposed names on this page if compute rents ever crack, because their equity sits under exactly this leverage.

→ CRWV · NBIS — the torque, on a leash

TIER 4Everything downstream of a funded buildout

$500B of new credit is a demand underwrite for the whole physical stack — power, grid equipment, cooling, optics, memory. Every layer we've mapped gets a longer runway when the money keeps arriving.

03

The load-bearing assumption

THE CRUXChips are not toll roads

A toll road earns for 50 years. A GPU's competitive half-life is 2–3 years — chip generations now refresh annually, and Amazon just cut its server useful-life assumption from six years to five. Lending 5–7 year money against fast-depreciating collateral only works while compute rents stay high (they currently are — older GPUs have even been appreciating in rental markets). That is the single assumption holding up the entire structure. If rents crack, the residual values crack, the backstop gets called, and the "mortgage" comparison stops being a compliment.

THE HONEST LINE

Financialization is what booms do late, not early: when an asset can no longer grow on cash flows alone, finance turns it into collateral. That's not automatically doom — mortgages funded fifty years of American housing between crises — but the comparison the industry itself chose is an innovation that ended in 2008. The asymmetric trade is the arrangers, who collect fees in every scenario. The symmetric trade is everyone whose equity depends on GPUs holding their value. Know which one you own.
