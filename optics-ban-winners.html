FCC draft ban · Optical transceivers

The biggest buildup in history
just lost half its plugs.

Reuters broke it on August 4: the Trump administration is drafting an FCC ban on imports of new-model Chinese optical transceivers — the laser modules that connect every AI chip to every other AI chip inside a data center. China supplies roughly half the world's datacom transceivers. Primary target Zhongji Innolight alone holds ~27% of the global market and makes ~90% of its revenue outside China — mostly from US hyperscalers. This is the map of who takes that share.

01

The policy in four numbers

~27%
Innolight's share of the global data-center transceiver market — the single biggest vendor
~50%
Rough Chinese share of world datacom transceiver supply (Innolight + Eoptolink + peers)
1.6T
The ban covers NEW models — landing exactly on the 1.6-terabit generation Rubin-era clusters need
2026
FCC aims to publish this year via the Covered List — same mechanism used against Huawei, drones and inverters

Mechanics that matter: existing models are grandfathered, non-Chinese suppliers get exemptions, and the Covered List is company-based — moving production to Thailand doesn't get a listed company off it. Innolight was added to the Pentagon's military-companies list in June. The escalation ladder is visible.

03

The beneficiary tiers

TIER 1The share-takers — US & allied module makers

If Innolight's new models can't enter the US, hyperscalers re-source the exact same sockets. Coherent is the biggest American pure-play and is vertically integrated down to the indium-phosphide laser. Lumentum bought its way into hyperscaler module supply (Cloud Light) and is ramping 1.6T. Fabrinet is the quiet monopoly here: the neutral Thai factory that already builds NVIDIA's optics — nearly every non-Chinese brand routes production through it. Applied Opto is the lottery ticket: US production, thin margins, highest beta in the group.

→ COHR · LITE · FN · AAOI

TIER 2The arms dealers — components inside whoever wins

A transceiver is a DSP, lasers, drivers and detectors in a metal shell. Marvell sells the dominant PAM4 DSPs, Broadcom the rest — both currently ship into Innolight too, so a ban mostly reshuffles their customer list. MACOM and Semtech sell the analog guts to the non-Chinese makers who'd ramp. Real winners, but diluted — they win share shifts, not new demand.

→ MRVL · AVGO · MTSI · SMTC

TIER 3The workaround plays

Scarce, pricier optics accelerate the alternatives. Credo's active electrical cables replace short-reach optics inside the rack at a fraction of the cost. And every constraint on pluggable modules strengthens the long-game case for co-packaged optics — Broadcom's and NVIDIA's push to fuse the optics onto the switch silicon itself.

→ CRDO · AVGO · (NVDA co-packaged roadmap)

THE SLEEPERViavi — the one the market hasn't repriced

Every re-sourced socket needs qualification, and every new non-Chinese production line needs test gear. Viavi launched the first purpose-built 1.6T Ethernet test appliance (TestCenter D2) in March, bought Spirent's high-speed-Ethernet test business, and just printed +52% YoY revenue on hyperscaler demand — its CEO says 1.6T reaches parity with 800G next fiscal year. It sells the picks and shovels of the reshuffle itself, has zero Chinese module revenue to lose — and it's up just ~4% since the story broke, versus +14–32% for everything else on this page. Smaller torque than the module makers, but the only name here the ban trade hasn't already paid.

→ VIAV ($9.7B cap · ~20× forward earnings)

THE HONEST LINE

Three things the hype will skip. One: it's a draft — Reuters' own sources say the FCC could still modify or shelve it, and new-models-only means the bite lands with the 1.6T cycle, not tomorrow. Two: analysts are blunt that US makers lack the scale to replace Chinese vendors near-term — year one this mostly means higher transceiver prices (a margin tailwind for every name above, a capex tax on AWS, Microsoft, Google and Meta). Three: the easy trade already happened — +14–32% in four sessions, some of it earnings-driven. This is a supply reshuffle, not new demand. Size accordingly.
