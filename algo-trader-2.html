Every link from the reel. For each skill: a free video to learn it, a free video to practice it, a hands-on project with a copy-paste AI prompt, and the open-source GitHub repo the pros use.

How to use this page

  1. Watch the Learn video for a skill. Just watch, no notes needed.
  2. Do the practice project under it. Copy the prompt into ChatGPT or Claude and let the AI walk you through it step by step.
  3. Open the GitHub repo once your project works, to see how the pros build the same thing at scale.
  4. Paper trade only. Every project here uses fake money or past data. Never point a bot you just built at real money.

New here? Start with 01 Day trading and 02 Stock research (no code needed), then 04, 05 and 03. Haven't seen Part 1 yet? Start with Part 1 ↗. You can use Google Colab (free, runs in your browser) for every coding project, so there's nothing to install.

Practice project · 5 trading days

Your project

Write a real day-trading plan, then follow it for 5 days on a paper account (fake money) and journal every trade.

  1. Open a free TradingView paper trading account (the practice video shows how).
  2. Paste the prompt below into ChatGPT or Claude to write your one-page plan and journal.
  3. Take at most 1–3 paper trades a day, only when your plan's setup appears, and journal each one.
  4. Advanced: install day-trader-skill in Claude to scan, size and time trades for you. Our free setup guide ↗
Copy into ChatGPT or Claude
I'm a beginner learning day trading on a paper trading account (fake money). Help me write a one-page trading plan.

Ask me questions first, then include:
1. Which stocks I'll trade (liquid, high volume, price above $10).
2. ONE entry setup, explained simply (for example an opening range breakout).
3. Where my stop-loss goes and how I size each trade so I never risk more than 1% of my account.
4. Daily limits: max 3 trades, stop for the day after 2 losses, flat by the close.
5. A simple journal template (setup, entry, exit, followed the rules yes/no, lesson).

Don't recommend specific stocks to buy. At the end, quiz me with 5 questions on my own rules.

You're done when: you've journaled 5 paper days and followed your rules on every trade, win or lose.

Practice project · about 1 hour

Your project

Write a one-page research note on a company you already know, the way an analyst would.

  1. Pick a company whose products you use (Costco, Apple, Nike…).
  2. Paste the prompt below into ChatGPT or Claude (turn on web search so it can read the latest annual report).
  3. Check two of its numbers yourself against the company's annual report or investor page.
  4. Advanced: add Anthropic's financial-services plugins to Claude and run its equity research agent on the same company.
Copy into ChatGPT or Claude
Act as an equity research analyst teaching a beginner. Help me write a one-page research note on [COMPANY].

Include:
1. What it sells and who pays for it.
2. How it makes money: its main revenue segments.
3. Three key numbers from the latest annual report (revenue growth, operating margin, debt) and what each one means.
4. The bull case and the bear case, 3 bullets each.
5. The 3 things to watch at the next earnings report.

Use the company's latest annual report, say where each number comes from, and flag anything you're unsure about. Don't tell me whether to buy.

You're done when: you have a one-page note you could explain to a friend in 2 minutes.

Practice project · about 1 hour

Your project

Forecast a month of prices with Google's AI, then check how wrong it was. That's the real lesson.

  1. Open Google Colab (free, runs in your browser).
  2. Paste the prompt below into ChatGPT or Claude and run each cell it gives you.
  3. Compare the AI forecast with what actually happened, and with a dumb “same as yesterday” forecast.
  4. Advanced: try Kronos on the same stock to forecast whole candles.
Copy into ChatGPT or Claude
Teach me to use Google's TimesFM forecasting model in Google Colab. I'm a beginner.

1. Download 2 years of daily closing prices for SPY with yfinance.
2. Hold out the last 30 days, and forecast those 30 days with TimesFM.
3. Plot the forecast against what actually happened, and calculate the average error.
4. Compare it with a naive forecast that just repeats the last price.

Explain every step simply. Finish by explaining in plain English why price forecasts are usually wrong, and what they can still be useful for.

You're done when: you can say whether TimesFM beat the naive forecast on your test, and by how much.

Practice project · about 45 minutes

Your project

Run a hedge fund investment committee on one stock, then judge which arguments hold up.

  1. Pick one large company you know.
  2. Paste the prompt below into ChatGPT or Claude. No code needed.
  3. Read the debate and write down which side convinced you, and why.
  4. Advanced: install TradingAgents (the practice video shows how) to run the real multi-agent desk. It needs an API key from an AI provider, and each run costs a little.
Copy into ChatGPT or Claude
Simulate a hedge fund investment committee for [TICKER]. Play these roles in order:

1. A fundamentals analyst
2. A news and sentiment analyst
3. A technical analyst
4. A bull researcher and a bear researcher, who argue with each other
5. A risk manager, who sets a maximum position size and explains why

Give each role 4–6 bullet points, and have each one say what data would make it more confident. End with a summary table of the arguments, and tell me which argument is weakest and why.

This is for learning how a trading desk thinks, not a recommendation.

You're done when: you can explain how a desk turns five different opinions into one decision.

Practice project · about 1 hour

Your project

Turn a vague trading idea into exact rules, then test them on 10 years of real data.

  1. Pick a simple idea from the learn video (for example: buy strong stocks after a pullback).
  2. Paste the prompt below into ChatGPT or Claude. Let it question you until the rules are exact.
  3. Run the code in Google Colab and read the results table.
  4. Advanced: give the same idea to Vibe-Trading in plain English and compare its backtest with yours.
Copy into ChatGPT or Claude
Help me turn a trading idea into exact, testable rules. My idea: [DESCRIBE YOUR IDEA].

First, ask me questions until the entry, exit, stop-loss and position size are 100% specific.

Then write Python (yfinance + backtesting.py, runnable in Google Colab) that backtests the rules on SPY and 4 large stocks from 2015 to today, with 0.1% costs per trade, and compares them with buy-and-hold.

Show the win rate, the average win vs the average loss, and the max drawdown. Finish with 3 reasons the result might not hold up in the future.

You're done when: your rules are so specific that two different people would place exactly the same trades.
