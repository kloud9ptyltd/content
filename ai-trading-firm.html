02

Build it — the exact setup

1

Clone and install

Python 3.12+ and five minutes. The repo is TauricResearch/TradingAgents on GitHub.

git clone https://github.com/TauricResearch/TradingAgents.git cd TradingAgents python3 -m venv .venv && .venv/bin/pip install .
2

One key runs every brain

Skip juggling OpenAI/Anthropic/Google keys — one OpenRouter key reaches all of them, including the cheap DeepSeek models that make runs cost cents. Create a .env in the repo folder:

OPENROUTER_API_KEY=sk-or-v1-your-key TRADINGAGENTS_LLM_PROVIDER=openrouter TRADINGAGENTS_DEEP_THINK_LLM=deepseek/deepseek-chat TRADINGAGENTS_QUICK_THINK_LLM=deepseek/deepseek-chat TRADINGAGENTS_MAX_DEBATE_ROUNDS=2 TRADINGAGENTS_MAX_RISK_ROUNDS=2
3

Free macro data (optional but worth it)

Stock data comes from yfinance with no key. Add a free FRED key (fred.stlouisfed.org) to the .env as FRED_API_KEY=... and the macro analyst gets real Federal Reserve data — rates, inflation, labor.

4

Run the firm

Launch from the repo folder (that's where the .env is found). Pick a ticker and date, then watch each desk light up live in the terminal.

.venv/bin/python -m cli.main
5

Upgrade the brain when it matters

Swap one line to put a frontier model in the chair — same firm, sharper analysts, ~20× the price.

TRADINGAGENTS_DEEP_THINK_LLM=openai/gpt-5.6-sol
6

A real run, start to finish — our NVDA example

The CLI walks you through a few prompts, then every desk lights up live in the terminal. Here's the exact frontier run behind this page — NVDA, as of 2026-08-10:

Step 1 · Ticker NVDA Step 2 · Analysis date 2026-08-10 Step 3 · Output language English (default) Step 4 · Analysts team market · social · news · fundamentals (space toggles, enter = done) ✓ Research depth 2 debate / 2 risk rounds (from .env) ✓ LLM provider openrouter · openai/gpt-5.6-sol (from .env) Analyst Team market · sentiment · news · fundamentals completed Research Team bull · bear · research manager completed Trading Team trader completed Action: Sell — "Trim NVDA to an underweight allocation: at $223.96 the 17.37× forward P/E leans on unfavourable near-term risk/reward."

⚠ Heads-up on Reddit: the social/sentiment analyst pulls Reddit's public RSS, which now rate-limits aggressively — our run threw HTTP 429 · Too Many Requests on r/stocks and r/investing and backed off. Sentiment still finishes (it leans on the other sources), but for reliable social data you'll want a proper scraper wired in as an extra tool — e.g. an Apify Reddit actor — rather than the bare RSS feed. Same goes for any source that gates bots.

SCOREBOARDEarly days — but the $3 desk is winning

Marking to market: NVDA closed $223.96 on the analysis date, and within two sessions it had pulled back to ~$217.50 — down about 2.9%. The frontier desk's Underweight is ahead; the budget desk's Buy is underwater. The kicker: the cheap model's stop-loss at $224 — placed at spot — would have stopped its own trade out almost immediately. Full disclaimer: this is one ticker, one run, a few days of price action — an anecdote, not a track record. Direction being right doesn't validate the process any more than the flip invalidated it. But if you're keeping score at home: expensive brain 1, cheap brain 0.

04

The honest findings

WORKSThe process is genuinely impressive to watch

Four analysts pull real data (my run cited NVIDIA's actual fundamentals — the multi-trillion market cap, forward P/E, PEG, margins, the debt load), then a bull and a bear argue the case before a trader and risk team weigh in. As a research workflow demo, it's the best free show in AI finance.

→ film the terminal. the debate IS the content.

FAILSIt placed a stop-loss at the current price

The portfolio manager recommended a stop at a level that was, at that moment, roughly where NVDA was trading. Why? The technical analyst had flagged that number as resistance — and the cheap model recycled it as a stop without ever checking it against spot. A human risk manager catches that in one glance. This is the cheap-model tax: numbers get moved around confidently but not sanity-checked.

MISSINGNo price target — by design

No agent in the chain does valuation math. There's no DCF, no comps model, no earnings build. The framework outputs a desk-style decision (buy/hold/sell + sizing + risk framing), not an analyst's target. If you want "12-month PT $X", this tool structurally cannot give it to you — no matter which model you pay for.

THE FLIPSwap the brain, flip the verdict

Held everything fixed — ticker, date, data, agents — and swapped only the model. The budget desk said Overweight. The frontier desk said Underweight. When the same "research process" reaches opposite conclusions depending on which brain you rent, the verdict isn't coming from the data — it's coming from the model's temperament, wearing a research firm as a costume. (To be fair to the machines: two runs on the same cheap model DID agree with each other — the flip is systematic model personality, not random noise. And humans at different desks disagree on NVDA too. But nobody sells you a human desk as an algorithm.)

THE HONEST LINE

This is a research toy with a firm-shaped costume — brilliant for learning how a desk reasons, dangerous if you trade its output raw. It has no valuation engine, its risk checks are only as sharp as the model you pay for, its confidence never wavers even when its numbers should — and as my A/B showed, its final verdict can flip 180° based purely on which model you plug in. Treat every verdict as one intern's opinion, not an answer. Educational content only — not financial advice.
