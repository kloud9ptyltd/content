The Puzzles — real interview questions

The firms don't test memory — they test how you reason under uncertainty. Try before you open the answer.

Jane Street · probability

100 passengers board a full flight. The first sits in a random seat; everyone else takes their own seat if free, else a random one. What's the probability the 100th passenger gets their own seat?

Show the answer
Exactly ½. The last open seat can only ever be seat 1 or seat 100. Every random pick is even-handed between those two, and the process ends the moment one is taken — so they're a coin flip. Works for 100 passengers or 100 million. They want the symmetry argument, not the number.
Citadel · expected value

You flip a fair coin until it lands heads. What's the expected number of flips?

Show the answer
2. Let E be the expected flips. With probability ½ you're done in 1; with probability ½ you've "wasted" one and start over: E = 1 + ½·E → E = 2. Geometric-distribution intuition in one line.
Optiver · mental math

A bat and a ball cost $1.10 together. The bat costs $1.00 more than the ball. How much is the ball?

Show the answer
$0.05. Not $0.10 — that's the trap. Ball = x, bat = x+1, so 2x+1 = 1.10 → x = 0.05. Firms use fast questions like this to see if you slow down when your gut is confidently wrong.
