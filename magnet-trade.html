The Magnet Trade

The President said "do magnets." Here's who benefits.

At a Pennsylvania investment event, Trump went off-script and told the room — twice, then a third time — to get into the rare-earth magnet business. We pulled the transcript, the supply-chain math, and his own trust's disclosed trades. This is the full picture.

What he actually said — verbatim

"Magnets. Do magnets, okay? I'll tell you how to make money. Do magnets… I guarantee it's going to be successful. It's one thing we don't [have] — for some reason it's not complex, not hard. We have the material. For some reason they just don't do it. We need magnets."

— Pennsylvania energy & investment event, August 2026. He also called on Blackstone's Jon Gray to fund "a couple of bucks" into the magnet business.

🧲 Why magnets, and why now

Rare-earth permanent magnets (NdFeB) are the muscle inside EV motors, drones, missiles, wind turbines and robots. China controls roughly 90% of global magnet production — and has already used export controls as leverage. Washington's answer: build a domestic mine-to-magnet chain, whatever it costs. The Pentagon isn't subsidizing this market — it's becoming a shareholder in it.

The magnet stocks — four ways to play it
MPNYSE

MP Materials — the chosen one

The only American mine-to-magnet chain: Mountain Pass mine → Fort Worth magnet plant. The DoD is its largest shareholder with a price floor under its key product; Apple signed a supply deal; magnet sales began ramping H2 2026. Q1 revenue +49%. The premium is real — so is the moat.

USARNASDAQ

USA Rare Earth — the challenger

Building the rival magnet plant in Stillwater, Oklahoma. How do you know it matters? MP Materials is suing them over magnet technology and poached engineers. Companies don't sue irrelevant competitors. Higher risk, earlier stage, more torque.

UUUUNYSE-A

Energy Fuels — the feedstock

Every magnet plant needs NdPr oxide — Energy Fuels produces it at White Mesa, the operating US facility, alongside its uranium business. The picks-and-shovels way in: if magnet capacity grows, someone has to feed it.

METCNASDAQ

Ramaco Resources — the sleeper

Pulling rare earths out of coal deposits in Wyoming — the exact "coal waste into critical minerals" concept Trump praised in the same speech. Smallest, most speculative of the four, and the most direct echo of his words.

⚖️ Play it straight

These stocks already move on headlines and carry policy risk in both directions — subsidies can grow, and hype can fade. MP trades at a strategic premium; USAR and METC are pre-profit stories. Position sizes small, time horizons long, and read the filings — that's why the tracker is free.

NEW · The $3 billion follow-through — Aug 7, 2026

💰 What was actually announced

Days after "do magnets," the administration put money behind the words: ~$3 billion for critical minerals and battery projects, unveiled at a State Department roundtable of ~200 mining executives, with Trump declaring the US should be the world's "minerals superpower." The big checks: a $1.4B Defense Department conditional loan to battery-materials maker Sila Nanotechnologies, $400M to scandium miner Sunrise Energy Metals, $150M to magnet developer Niron Magnetics, and $80M for US mining schools — on top of 160+ mineral agreements worth ~$40B since he took office.

The catch nobody mentions: you can't buy any of the three direct recipients. Sila and Niron are private; Sunrise trades in Australia. The money lands on the sector — so the trade, if there is one, is the listed proxies. The tape already voted: in the two sessions after the announcement, MP +15% · NEM +11% · UUUU +11% · USAR +9% · PPTA +9% · NB +8% · LAC +8% · METC +6%.

The mining stocks — beyond the magnet four
NEMNYSE

Newmont — the one HIS trust bought 28 times

Here's the receipt the headlines miss: our tracker shows 28 disclosed purchases of Newmont in the President's trust filings (against 4 sells, buys up to the $250K–$500K bracket, most recent December 2025) — his most-bought mining stock by far. The world's largest gold miner is the trust's way of owning "mining" broadly. Now the policy is following the portfolio. Again.

PPTANASDAQ

Perpetua Resources — the Pentagon's antimony play

Idaho's Stibnite project is the only meaningful US source of antimony — a munitions-critical mineral China restricted for export — plus gold alongside. Already Pentagon-backed with a fast-tracked permit. The purest "defense supply chain" name on this page.

LACNYSE

Lithium Americas — the battery leg

$1.4B of the package went to battery materials — and Thacker Pass in Nevada is the biggest US lithium project, already carrying a multi-billion DoE loan and a GM partnership. If the administration keeps writing battery-chain checks, the largest domestic resource is the obvious desk it lands on.

NBNASDAQ

NioCorp — the lottery ticket

Nebraska's Elk Creek project covers niobium, scandium and titanium — and a $400M federal loan just went to a scandium producer, putting that obscure metal on the policy map. Small cap, pre-production, highest risk on this page — sized like a lottery ticket, not a position.

⚖️ Play this one straight too

These are conditional loans to mostly private recipients — the listed names benefit by association and policy direction, not by receiving checks. The sector already jumped on the news, so you're paying post-headline prices. Same rules as the magnet four: small sizes, long horizons, read the filings.
