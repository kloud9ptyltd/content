The Repricing Map

AI erased $285 billion of SaaS in 24 hours. Here's the decade map.

Feb 3, 2026 — the SaaSpocalypse: Salesforce, ServiceNow, HubSpot, Infosys and Wipro shed ~$285B of market value in a single day. Not on earnings. On a realization: AI agents do the work, but they don't buy seats. Every number below is from filings, earnings calls, and our own fundamentals database.

The mechanism — why per-seat pricing breaks
Per-seat pricing bills a proxy: humans logged in. Agents collapse the seat count while the work still gets done — and ~73% of SaaS companies are now rebuilding their pricing around usage or outcomes. In the transition, every renewal is a renegotiation where the buyer's alternative ("we'll have an agent build it") gets more credible each quarter. The alternative doesn't have to win to compress your price. It just has to be credible.

The three zones

KILL ZONE — thin per-seat tools

Single-workflow, prosumer-tier apps (the $30/month tier). Short distance from prompt to replacement; agents rebuild and migrate in days. Switching cost was the moat — AI deletes switching cost. Public proxy: design/creative incumbents that stayed seat-priced.

COMPRESSION ZONE — horizontal mid-market SaaS

CRM-lite, project tools, docs, e-sign. They survive, smaller: every renewal reprices against a credible agent-built alternative. Damage shows up as decelerating growth and squeezed sales efficiency long before gross margin moves — vendors cut their own costs with AI to defend the P&L. The exits: convert seats to consumption fast (HubSpot re-accelerated to +20% after its agentic pivot; Canva went AI-native and hit $4B ARR with 265M users, +47%) — the disruption is capturable by whoever moves first.

HOLDS — moats that aren't made of code

Data gravity & systems of record (the ledger IS the moat, and liability transfer is real in regulated work) · networks (you can't self-host a global edge — Cloudflare sits 2% off its high) · compute & its chokepoints (AWS +37%, Azure +43%, Google Cloud +82%; Microsoft's backlog $625B, +110%; Amazon raised capex to $220B citing memory costs) · observability of the mess everyone now runs (Datadog: $1B quarter, AI labs choosing it over building in-house).

The consulting preview — it already happened to services
Accenture: growth decelerated to 3–4%, Q3 bookings negative, 784K people being restructured around AI — while its AI bookings double. The accountability business survives; the price of accountability collapses. SaaS follows the same curve, one renewal cycle at a time.
Honesty box: the easy repricing already happened on Feb 3 — this is a decade thesis, not a trade. Total software spend likely rises (cheaper software → more of it); the losers are whoever stays attached to the old unit of pricing. Analysis, not investment advice. Do your own research.
