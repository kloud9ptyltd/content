02 · Try three real questions

Answer first, then reveal.

Jane Street section · page 37

Roll a die and you get paid the number. You may re-roll once. What is the fair price of this game?

Reveal answer
One roll alone is worth 3.5. With a free re-roll, you only re-roll when the first roll is below 3.5. So keep 4, 5 and 6, and re-roll 1, 2 and 3. That is ½ × 3.5 + ⅙ × (4 + 5 + 6) = $4.25.
Probability section · page 5

There are 1000 coins. 999 are fair and one has heads on both sides. You pick one at random and flip 10 heads in a row. What are the odds you are holding the rigged coin?

Reveal answer
It feels like a near-certainty, but it is not. Bayes' theorem gives 1 / (1 + 999/1024), which is about 50%. The rigged coin is rare enough that 999 fair coins produce a 10-heads streak about as often as the one rigged coin does.
Citadel section · page 44

100 passengers board a plane in order. Passenger 1 is drunk and sits in a random seat. Everyone else takes their own seat if it is free, and a random one if it is taken. What are the odds passenger 100 gets their own seat?

Reveal answer
Every random choice either ends the chaos (seat 1) or passes it on. Seats 1 and 100 are always equally likely to be the one that gets taken. The answer is exactly 1/2.
