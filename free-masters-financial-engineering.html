01 · How to apply

Four steps. One test.

Everything is online and free. The only real gate is a maths and Python test, so prepare for that first.

THE ONE THING

You need 75% or more on a 60-question entry test (2 hours, one sitting). You get two attempts. Free prep material for every topic is below ↓.

  • 1

    Check you qualify

    You need a completed bachelor's degree (any subject) and English. If your degree wasn't taught in English, you'll also need a test score: Duolingo 100, TOEFL iBT 71, IELTS 6.5 or PTE 50.

  • 2

    Create a free account

    Sign up on the WQU learning platform and start the MScFE application. One account per person.

    learn.wqu.edu/signup ↗
  • 3

    Pass the Quantitative Proficiency Test

    60 questions, 2 hours, 75% to pass. It covers algebra, linear algebra, calculus, differential equations, discrete maths, probability, statistics and basic Python. Fail the first try and you can retake it after 7 days. Fail twice and you wait six months.

    Free sample questions ↗   Free prep resources ↗
  • 4

    Upload your documents

    A government photo ID and the transcript of your degree (a scanned copy is fine to apply). Official transcripts are needed later, before your second course. Apply at least 3 weeks before the start date you want.

05 · Good to know

Honest notes.

It really is free. No tuition and no application fee. The only costs are your own: an English test if you need one, and official transcripts.
It's a big commitment. Plan for 20–25 hours a week for two years, and keep an 80% average to graduate.
Who it's best for: people with a maths, engineering, science or economics background who want quant skills and a master's without the $50K+ price tag.
Accredited by the DEAC, which is recognised by the US Department of Education. It's a newer name than a big-brand university, so your projects and skills do the talking.
Not available to nationals of Crimea, Cuba, Iran, North Korea or Syria.
