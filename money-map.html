01 — THE SPLIT

Where every dollar goes

Every month my take-home lands in five buckets. Essentials first — the bills that keep life running. Investing buys future freedom. Emergency is cash with one job. Lifestyle is the fun money I refuse to feel guilty about. Buffer catches the annual bills and broken things the other buckets forget.

03 — THE EMERGENCY FUND

Your safety net

The target is six months of your essentials, held in cash — a high-yield savings account, never invested. That fund is the difference between a job loss being a crisis and it being an inconvenient Tuesday. The 10% bucket feeds it until it's full; after that, that 10% rolls into Investing.

04 — THE RETIREMENT NUMBER

Your retirement number

My retirement number is 25 × my annual expenses — the yearly cost of my life, times twenty-five. At that point a 4% withdrawal covers me indefinitely, and work becomes a choice. I also track the cautious version: 30 ×. It takes longer to reach and it forgives worse markets.

05 — SOURCES

Where these numbers come from

01 · The split. It's a cousin of the 50/30/20 budget that Elizabeth Warren popularized in All Your Worth — 50% needs, 30% wants, 20% saving. I keep the 50, then move ten points out of wants and into saving, because I'd rather buy my freedom earlier than spend it at restaurants.

02 · The 4% rule. From the 1998 Trinity study (Retirement Savings: Choosing a Withdrawal Rate That Is Sustainable). Withdrawing 4% of a US stock-and-bond portfolio in year one, then adjusting for inflation, survived most 30-year retirements in the historical data. Twenty-five times annual expenses is the same idea from the other end. That's a US-market historical result — not a guarantee about the future.

03 · The six-month fund. A personal-finance convention, not a law: six months of essentials in cash, because that's roughly how long a serious job hunt takes. It assumes a reasonably stable income; if mine were shaky, I'd hold more.

All of these are rules of thumb for a normal, stable income — a starting map, not advice, and not a prediction of any market.
