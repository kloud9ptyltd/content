03 · The rules

How it teaches.

These are the rules written in the file, in the order you'll meet them.

  1. It splits the topic up

    Your topic becomes 4 to 10 concrete subskills.

  2. It tests you first

    Three to five questions, easy to hard, before it teaches anything.

  3. You get a skill map

    Every subskill gets a level from 0 to 5, shown as progress bars.

  4. One question at a time

    It asks, then stops and waits. No hints tacked on the end.

  5. It won't give you the answer

    Get it wrong and it asks a narrower question, so you find the mistake yourself. It only explains after two or three failed tries, then tests the same idea again from another angle.

  6. "Are you sure?"

    Get it right and it sometimes pushes back anyway, so you have to defend the answer.

  7. Levels are earned, and can be lost

    To move up you need two separate correct answers, one of them from memory. It also re-tests old subskills without warning, and a miss drops your level.

  8. A final exam

    When every subskill reaches level 4, it sets one mixed test covering everything. Fail and you go back to the lessons.

LevelWhat it means
0Unaware
1Heard of it
2Can follow along
3Can do it with help
4Can do it independently. This is the default finish line for every subskill.
5Can teach it and handle edge cases. Ask for "full mastery" to make this the target.

What a wrong answer looks like. This is an illustration I wrote to show the pattern, not a transcript:

TutorYou buy a call for $5 with a strike of $100. At expiry the stock is at $103. Did you make money?
YouYes, $3.
TutorNot quite. What did you pay to own that call?
You$5. Oh. I got $3 back on $5, so I lost $2.
TutorSo where does the stock need to finish for you to break even?
04 · Use it

Eight finance topics to start with.

Copy any line and send it. Narrow topics work best. A broad one gets split into branches and takes several sessions.

  1. Options basicsTeach me options: calls, puts and payoffs

    Start here if options are new to you.

  2. The GreeksTeach me the options Greeks

    Delta, gamma, theta and vega, and how they move together.

  3. Interview probabilityQuiz me on probability brainteasers for quant interviews

    Expected value, conditional probability and dice and card puzzles.

  4. BondsTeach me bond pricing, duration and convexity

    Why prices fall when rates rise, and by how much.

  5. Portfolio riskHelp me master portfolio risk: volatility, correlation and value at risk

    What actually drives the risk of a portfolio.

  6. ValuationTeach me DCF valuation

    Cash flows, discount rates and terminal value.

  7. Financial statementsDrill me on reading a cash flow statement

    Where the cash came from and where it went.

  8. Market microstructureTeach me how an order book works

    Bids, asks, spreads and what a market maker does.

Answer the diagnostic honestly. Guessing your way to a higher starting level only means it skips material you needed. If you don't know, say "I don't know".
05 · Controls

Six commands you can type anytime.

Type thisWhat it does
show progressShows your skill map with every level.
harder / easierChanges the difficulty of the questions.
just tell meGets you the answer to the current question only. The tutor goes back to asking after that.
save statePrints a short block with your topic, levels and weak spots.
resume from:Paste a saved block after it in a new chat to carry on where you stopped.
Save before you close the chat. A new chat doesn't remember your levels. Type save state at the end of each session, keep the block in your notes, and start the next session with resume from: and that block.
06 · Honest notes

Know what you're getting.

It's a tutor for any topic, not a trading tool. It doesn't pick stocks, give signals or contain any Jane Street trading knowledge. It's a set of teaching rules.
Jane Street published it; it hasn't said how it uses it. The repo describes the skill and nothing more, so treat it as a study method from a top firm, not a copy of its internal training.
The AI can still be wrong. The file controls how it teaches, not what it knows. Check anything important against a textbook or a course.
Better models make better tutors. The same file gives sharper questions on a stronger model, and a weaker one may drift back into lecturing.
It's slow on purpose. Expect a topic to take several sessions. That's the point of the method.
Only get the file from github.com/janestreet/agent_skills, never from copies or look-alike repos.
