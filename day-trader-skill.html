01 · The desk

Eleven skills. Eleven jobs. $0.

The pack is called day-trader-skill, built by Jake Schincariol (@opusjake) and released under the MIT licence. It's plain Python that reads free public data (Yahoo Finance prices, Nasdaq's symbol lists, SEC filings), so there's no API key, no signup and nothing to pay. It never trades on its own: every order needs your yes, and paper trading is the default.

  1. Scanner/trader-scanner

    Sweeps the market for breakouts, flags and oversold bounces.

  2. News desk/trader-news

    Headlines and SEC filings for the stocks you're watching.

  3. Entry timer/trader-entry

    Watches a setup candle by candle and calls the moment to buy.

  4. Exit planner/trader-exit

    Sets your stop-loss and profit target before you're in.

  5. Time Machine/trader-timemachine

    Replays the rest of the day 10,000 times and gives you the odds.

  6. Position sizer/trader-budget

    How many shares fit your account and your max loss.

  7. Risk manager/trader-risk

    Blocks any trade that breaks your limits.

  8. Order desk/trader-broker

    Sends approved orders to your broker, paper by default.

  9. Alerts/trader-alerts

    Pings you at your stop, your target, and before the close.

  10. Earnings analyst/trader-analyst

    Reads a company's latest reported numbers from the SEC.

  11. Congress tracker/trader-pelosi

    New stock-trade disclosures filed by members of Congress.

02 · Setup

Install it in two minutes.

These skills run inside Claude Code, Anthropic's coding assistant. Already have it? Jump to step 2.

  • 1

    Get Claude Code, in either form. You need a paid Claude plan (Pro or Max, or an Anthropic API account). The free plan doesn't include Claude Code.

  1. Option A · Claude Desktop (easiest)

    No terminal needed. Download the Claude desktop app for Mac or Windows, sign in, and click the Code tab at the top of the sidebar. The app itself is free, but the Code tab needs a Pro or Max plan; on the free plan it asks you to upgrade. Then go straight to step 2.

  2. Option B · Claude Code in the terminal

    Open a terminal (Mac: the Terminal app; Windows: PowerShell) and paste the line for your computer, below.

Mac · Linux
curl -fsSL https://claude.ai/install.sh | bash
Windows · PowerShell
irm https://claude.ai/install.ps1 | iex

The installer shows no progress bar, so give it a minute. Then open a new terminal window, type claude and press Enter. A browser window opens for you to log in with your Claude account. To check it worked, claude --version prints a version number. Official guide: code.claude.com/docs/en/setup.

Also needed: Python 3.8 or newer, which the skills use for their calculations. Macs usually have it (if not, macOS offers to install it the first time it's needed). On Windows, install it from python.org and tick "Add python.exe to PATH". Or just ask Claude Code: "Check I have Python 3.8+ and help me install it if not."
  • 2

    Install the skills. In Claude Desktop or Claude Code (type claude in your terminal), paste this in and send it:

Paste into Claude
https://github.com/Jakeschincariol/day-trader-skill

install skill

Or install it yourself in Claude Code, either as a plugin (commands get a /day-trader-skill: prefix) or by copying the folders (short names like /trader-scanner):

Claude Code · plugin
/plugin marketplace add Jakeschincariol/day-trader-skill
/plugin install day-trader-skill@day-trader-skill
Terminal · Mac / Linux
git clone https://github.com/Jakeschincariol/day-trader-skill.git
cp -r day-trader-skill/trader-* ~/.claude/skills/
One extra step for the news and earnings skills: the SEC asks every tool to say who's calling. Tell Claude "set my SEC user agent to Your Name you@email.com", or set the SEC_USER_AGENT environment variable yourself.
03 · Your first trade

Run the desk in order.

Each skill passes its work to the next, so go top to bottom. Plain English works.

  • 1

    Find a setup. "Scan for opening-range breakouts in NVDA, TSLA, AAPL, MSFT, META, AMD."

  • 2

    Check the news on the one you like. "Any news on NVDA today?"

  • 3

    Time the entry. "Watch NVDA and tell me when it triggers."

  • 4

    Plan the exit before you buy. "Set my stop and target."

  • 5

    Run the Time Machine. "Replay this trade. What are the odds?"

  • 6

    Size it. "I have $5,000 and want to risk $50 max. How many shares?"

  • 7

    Risk check, then you decide. If it passes and you still want it, the order desk can send it to a paper account. You type yes. Nothing happens without you.

04 · The Time Machine

How to read the odds.

You give it a trade: an entry, a stop-loss and a target. It rebuilds the rest of that trading day 10,000 times, each time stitching together real 5-minute moves the stock made at that time of day over the last ~60 sessions. Every replay ends one of three ways:

Target
Price hit your profit target first. A win.
Stop
Price hit your stop-loss first. A small, planned loss.
Neither
Still open at the close. The closing price decides.

The part most people miss: how often you need to win depends on your target. With a target twice as far as your stop (the default, "2R"), winning about 1 in 3 breaks even. With a target the same distance as your stop ("1R"), you need about 1 in 2. The verdict line does that maths for you:

The verdict saysWhat it means
Leans / strongly toward the targetWins more often than you need. The odds are on your side.
Close to break-even (or coin flip)No real edge either way.
Leans / strongly toward the stopLoses more often than you can afford. Think twice.

It also re-runs the same setup on every real day in the last ~60 sessions and shows how often it worked. If the simulation and the real history disagree, trust neither blindly.

05 · Beyond the reel

I tested it on two months of Nvidia.

I went back through every Nvidia breakout from 31 Aug to 23 Oct 2026. On each day, the Time Machine only saw data from before that day, exactly as if you'd asked it live. Then I checked what really happened.

Across 12 big stocks (Nvidia, Tesla, Apple, Microsoft, Amazon, Meta, Google, AMD, Broadcom, Palantir, Micron, Oracle; 267 breakouts), the pattern held. It said "go" just 7 times. The trades it flagged as leaning toward the stop lost money on average, and the ones it called break-even came out roughly flat.

What this does and doesn't show: the Time Machine is good at talking you out of bad trades. I did not find that it picks winners. Two months and one strategy is a small sample, and trading costs and slippage aren't included.
06 · Is it safe?

What I checked before installing.

Read the code. Plain Python using only the built-in library, so no hidden packages get installed.
Only public data sites. Yahoo Finance, Nasdaq, NYSE, the SEC, the House Clerk and Stooq. Nothing else.
Can't trade by itself. Orders need your typed yes, then Claude asks again. Paper trading is the default.
MIT licensed, with a test suite in the repo.
It's brand new (released 20 Oct 2026), so expect rough edges. Only install it from github.com/Jakeschincariol/day-trader-skill, never from copies or look-alike repos.
07 · Before you trade

Know what you're getting.

  1. A simulation, not a forecast

    It only knows the last ~60 days. Earnings, big news or a market shock can do things those days never did.

  2. Free data is delayed

    Yahoo prices can lag, so treat live signals as a guide, not a trigger.

  3. Long trades only

    The entry and exit skills plan buys, not short sales.

  4. Start on paper

    Run it for a few weeks with fake money before a real dollar goes in.
