Uber became a verb.
The stock hasn't caught up.

"Just Uber there." Nobody says hail a rideshare — the brand ate the behavior, the way Google ate search. Behind the verb: 208 million people use it every month, they spend more per head every year, and the company now throws off ten billion dollars a year in free cash. Meanwhile the stock trades sideways because the market is arguing about robotaxis. That argument is my entry.

The bear case · argued honestly

The robotaxi question is real.

The whole debate is one question: when cars drive themselves, does Uber still own the customer? Waymo ended its Phoenix partnership and went direct — in San Francisco it has taken a real bite of the rideshare market. If AV operators keep their own apps and their own riders, Uber's take rate shrinks toward that of a booking site. Add the capital Uber is committing to fleet partnerships, and the bear case writes itself: growth slows, margins compress, the multiple follows.

What I watch, not what I feel

The bull's counter-evidence so far: cities with AVs on Uber (Austin, Atlanta) are among its fastest-growing markets — autonomy grows the pie faster than it steals slices. The unresolved variable is the AV take rate. That's the number that decides which scenario below we live in.

The plan · my entry zone

Where I want to buy it.

The monthly chart has one structure that matters: the $52–67 demand zone — the shelf where buyers showed up in 2021, 2023, and again on every panic since. Two independent lines of reasoning point at the same shelf: it's where the chart says demand lives, and it's roughly what the Delivery + advertising businesses alone are worth — meaning down there, the market hands you the entire Mobility business at a discount and the robotaxi argument for free.

The tripwires · what changes my mind
▸

AV take rate — if autonomy rides on Uber at healthy economics, the bull case compounds. If operators go direct in city after city, respect the bear.

▸

Rider + spend growth — the two charts above. The thesis dies the quarter both flatten, whatever the robotaxi headlines say.

▸

Fleet capex — billions committed to robotaxi hardware is the quiet threat to the FCF story. Watch capex guidance, not keynote demos.
