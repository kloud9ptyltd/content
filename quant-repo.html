01

What's in it.

Built by cybergeekgyan, the repo cybergeekgyan/Quant-Developers-Resources was created 16 October 2023 and last pushed 15 August 2026 — it's active, and it's earned 3,865 stars and 462 forks along the way. But the README's folder count is stale: it once advertised more top-level entries than exist today. We counted what's actually in the tree right now — 33 top-level folders. Of those, 7 are empty placeholders (Matlab, Operating Systems, Optimization Theory, Parallel Computing, PyTorch, Signal Processing, Statsmodels — a README title and nothing else), roughly 19 are single-README bullet-list stubs with no outbound links, and the real content sits in five folders: Artificial Intelligence (22 files), Mathematics (11), Projects (9 strategy write-ups), Python (6 books), and FPGA (4 ebooks). This page treats the repo as a map, not a library — the value is in the README's lists (rounds, books, courses, firms), and the thin folders point you somewhere else.

03

The 6-week path.

The README gives you lists. It doesn't give you an order. This is the order, built from the folders and courses that actually have content.

Week 1

Probability foundations

Open: Mathematics folder (Probability Theory + Statistics) + MIT Probability (6.041)-style lectures + Mosteller's 50 problems.

Done when: you can do 10 Mosteller problems cold.
Week 2

Puzzles & mental math

Open: BrainTeasers_Puzzles folder + cut-the-knot.org + brainstellar.com.

Done when: a Heard on the Street chapter feels routine.
Week 3

Stochastic calculus & pricing

Open: MIT Financial Mathematics + IIT Kanpur Stochastic Calculus playlists + Financial Theory folder.

Done when: you can derive Black-Scholes from Itô.
Week 4

Markets & market-making

Open: Poker Theory + Game Theory folders + Natenberg + Harris.

Done when: you can quote a two-sided market on a coin-flip game and explain your edge.
Week 5

Code

Open: C++ or Python folder (pick the track for your target role) + the Hilpisch playlist (Python) or Effective Modern C++ (dev).

Done when: you've shipped one project from the Projects folder.
Week 6

Apply

Open: QuantCompaniesList + QuantCompetition_Fellowships + ResumeTemplates.

Done when: 10 applications and 2 fellowship/insight-week entries are out.

The Claude tutor.

Three self-contained prompts. Paste any of them into Claude or ChatGPT as-is, then follow its lead.

Quiz me like a quant screen
Turns any chapter or playlist into a 10-question timed round.
You are a quant-interview screener. I will paste a book chapter, README bullet list, or playlist description from a quant-finance repo here: [PASTE CONTENT HERE]. From it, write exactly 10 questions covering rounds 1-3 of quant hiring: mental math/speed arithmetic, brainteasers/puzzles, and probability & statistics/Bayes. Mix types — 3 numeric/mental-math, 3 classic puzzle/logic, 4 probability/stats. Ask them one at a time, numbered 1-10. Give me a 60-second timer per question (tell me to answer before revealing the next one — you don't need a real clock, just don't show the answer early). After each answer, grade it right/wrong/partial, explain the correct reasoning in 2-3 sentences, and show a running score out of 10 at the top of every reply. At question 10, give my final score, flag my 2 weakest categories, and recommend one repo folder or book to drill next.
Turn this playlist into a schedule
Builds a day-by-day study plan with weekly self-tests.
You are a self-study scheduler for quant finance. I will paste a YouTube playlist URL or a list of video titles here: [PASTE PLAYLIST URL OR TITLE LIST HERE], my start date here: [START DATE], and how many weeks I have here: [NUMBER OF WEEKS]. Build a day-by-day plan assuming 2 hours a day, 5 days a week. For each day, list which video(s) or topic block to cover and a one-line goal. At the end of every week, write a 5-question self-test checkpoint (write the actual questions). If the playlist is longer than the timeframe allows at 2 hrs/day, tell me explicitly how many extra days I need rather than silently compressing it. Output as a markdown table: Week | Day | Topic | Goal | Checkpoint. End with one paragraph on what I'll be able to do by the final day.
Mock market-making round
A 15-minute live betting/quoting game with a scorecard.
You are a market-making interviewer running a timed betting game. Pick one game yourself — coin flips, dice, or card draws — and state the exact rules and payout structure in 3 sentences. Give me 15 minutes total (tell me when roughly half the time and 2 minutes remain; you don't need a real clock, just track turns and announce it). Each round, state the scenario, then ask me to quote a two-sided market (bid/ask) or a bet size. After each of my answers, tell me whether my quote or sizing was too wide, too tight, or mispriced relative to true edge, and why, in 2-3 sentences. Track my running P&L in fake units and show it each round. At time's end, give a scorecard: realized P&L, average spread width, and one sentence on whether I was over- or under-sizing my edge, plus one concrete thing to fix next time.

What the repo is missing.

The honest gaps — and where we point you instead.

  • No license file on the repo — assume "all rights reserved" until the author says otherwise, and ask before reusing anything verbatim.
  • The CTC (salary) column in the India firms table is entirely empty — nothing to compare against; check each firm's own career page.
  • The "10 Youtube playlists" table is mislabeled — it actually has 21 rows. We resolved all of them in Section 04.
  • Every course link in the README is a LinkedIn-shortener redirect with no preview — we replaced every one with its final, verified URL.
  • 7 folders are empty placeholders (Matlab, Operating Systems, Optimization Theory, Parallel Computing, PyTorch, Signal Processing, Statsmodels) and roughly 19 more are single-README stubs with no links — go to Section 04's courses or primary sources like MIT OpenCourseWare instead.
  • Firm and fellowship links go stale fast (WorldQuant, Tower Research, and the Citadel Trading Invitational all blocked our automated checker) — when in doubt, go to the firm's own career page directly rather than trusting an old link.
