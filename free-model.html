The Free Model
Cloud9 thesis · contrarian

The AI model is going to be free. Here's where the money actually goes.

Everyone's betting on who has the best model. History says that's the wrong bet. AI is following the exact playbook that turned proprietary software, encyclopedias and steel into commodities — the thing everyone's paying for today races to zero, and the profit migrates one layer over. Here's the pattern, the receipts, and where it points.

Input (compute) firm-to-rising, output (tokens) collapsing. The gap is throughput — each GPU now spits out far more tokens, and competition passes it to users. Cheaper intelligence isn't a blip. It's the trend.

The disruptor — good enough, and free

Chinese open-weight models (DeepSeek, Qwen, Kimi) already do 50–80% of everyday tasks — running free on your own hardware, or at 1/5–1/10 the price of frontier models. For most users, and even most power users, that's good enough. Enterprises keep frontier for the hard 20%. Sound familiar? It should.

This already happened — five times
Proprietary Unix → LinuxFree "good enough" OS ate the market. Sun collapsed. Red Hat got rich selling support & reliability on top.
Britannica → WikipediaFree-and-good-enough killed a $2,000 product that was more accurate. Quality didn't save it.
Steel → minimillsIncumbents "retreated upmarket" to the complex stuff — a comfortable-feeling death march as minimills climbed.
Mainframe → PCThe box commoditized. Money moved to chips (Intel) and software (Microsoft).
Telecom bandwidthPer-bit price → ~0. Selling the pipe got brutal; selling what runs through it got huge.

The one law under all of them

Conservation of Attractive Profits: when a layer commoditizes, profit doesn't vanish — it migrates to the adjacent layer that's still scarce. Every time. The commodity layer's revenue can even keep growing (more usage) while its margins get gutted. Great for integrated players, fatal for anyone selling only the commodity.

Where the money goes in AI

↓ Below — the scarce input

Chips, memory, energy, data-center capacity. The GPU chart proves it: Blackwell firm-to-rising while tokens collapse. This is the safest profit pool — the picks-and-shovels layer stays scarce.

↑ Above — the "Red Hat of intelligence"

The application & agent layer: reliability, security, compliance, vertical workflows, distribution, and the last 20% for high-stakes code/legal/medical/finance. Monetized by subscriptions and seats — which decouple revenue from per-token cost. That's where switching costs and real margin live.

✕ The middle — the raw token

Selling commodity tokens by the million is the Sun-Microsystems seat. Even the labs know it — that's why usage limits keep getting extended, not tightened: subsidize usage, defend the workflow, accept margin compression on the commodity.

⚠ Read carefully

This is a framework and our opinion — not a stock call. Open weights aren't literally "free" at enterprise scale (that's the gap Red Hat monetized), a real capability frontier can hold a premium in a shrinking niche, and timing commoditization is hard. Educational only, not financial advice. Do your own research.
