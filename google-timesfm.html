On August 31, Google Research dropped TimesFM 3.0 — a foundation model that does for time series what GPT did for text. Feed it any sequence of numbers — a stock price, sales data, crypto, your own metrics — and it forecasts what comes next, zero-shot, no training. This page: what it is, what its predecessor said about NVDA, and the exact code to run 3.0 yourself in 5 minutes, free.

What dropped · Aug 31, 2026

The release, in numbers.

330Mparameters — small enough to run on a free Colab CPU in seconds.
1T+time points in its training corpus, from real-world and synthetic series.
#1ranked pre-trained foundation model on all three major forecasting benchmarks — GIFT-Eval, FEV-Bench and the Time leaderboard — ahead of Amazon's Chronos-2 and Datadog's Toto 2.0.
NEWnative multivariate forecasting — earlier versions could only look at one series at a time; 3.0 forecasts related series together and can use side information, including events it knows are coming.
1 PASSthe whole forecast horizon comes out in a single forward pass, with 9 quantile bands so you get uncertainty, not just a line.

Run it yourself · free · ~5 minutes

TimesFM 3.0 on any stock, in Colab.

1
Open a fresh notebook

Go to colab.research.google.com → New notebook. Free tier is plenty — no GPU needed.

2
Paste this into one cell and run it

First run downloads the model (~1.2 GB) from Google's official Hugging Face repo, then forecasts in seconds. Change TICKER to anything yfinance knows.

!pip install -q timesfm yfinance import numpy as np, pandas as pd, yfinance as yf import matplotlib.pyplot as plt from timesfm3 import TimesFM3Evaluator, ModelConfig TICKER, HORIZON = "NVDA", 30 close = yf.download(TICKER, period="3y", auto_adjust=True, progress=False)["Close"].squeeze().dropna() context = close.values.astype(np.float32)[-1000:] model = TimesFM3Evaluator(ModelConfig( checkpoint_path="google/timesfm-3.0-pytorch", device="cpu")) out = list(model.predict_batch(contexts=[context], horizon=HORIZON, return_quantiles=True))[0] median, q10, q90 = out.quantiles[:,4], out.quantiles[:,0], out.quantiles[:,8] dates = pd.bdate_range(close.index[-1] + pd.Timedelta(days=1), periods=HORIZON) plt.figure(figsize=(12,6)) plt.plot(close.index[-120:], close.values[-120:], color="w", label="actual") plt.plot(dates, median, color="cyan", label="TimesFM 3.0 median") plt.fill_between(dates, q10, q90, color="cyan", alpha=.18, label="q10–q90") plt.gca().set_facecolor("#06060e"); plt.gcf().set_facecolor("#06060e") plt.legend(); plt.title(f"{TICKER} — TimesFM 3.0 · {HORIZON}-day forecast", color="w"); plt.tick_params(colors="w"); plt.show() print(f"Last close ${close.iloc[-1]:.2f} -> 30-day median " f"${median[-1]:.2f} ({(median[-1]/close.iloc[-1]-1)*100:+.1f}%)")
3
Read it like a quant, not a fortune teller

The median line is the model's central guess — the band is the honest part. A wide band means the model itself is telling you the future is uncertain. No forecasting model, Google's included, predicts the market reliably — that's precisely why the uncertainty bands are the feature to respect.

Bonus · TradingView

Draw the forecast on your chart.

This is my favourite part. The script below draws the TimesFM forecast into the future of a live TradingView chart — the gold cone is the q10–q90 band, the dashed gold line is the model's 30-day price target (its median endpoint), with the % move on the label. Open Pine Editor (bottom panel of TradingView) → paste → Add to chart. That's it.

Make it yours: the four array.from(...) rows are my Sep 1 NVDA run (TimesFM 2.5). To regenerate them for any ticker, add this to the end of your Colab cell and paste what it prints over those four rows:

j = lambda v: ", ".join(f"{x:.2f}" for x in v) print("var int[] f_t = array.from(" + ", ".join(str(int(d.timestamp()*1000)) for d in dates) + ")") print("var float[] f_q50 = array.from(" + f"{close.iloc[-1]:.2f}, " + j(median) + ")") print("var float[] f_q10 = array.from(" + f"{close.iloc[-1]:.2f}, " + j(q10) + ")") print("var float[] f_q90 = array.from(" + f"{close.iloc[-1]:.2f}, " + j(q90) + ")")

The fine print, in plain English

What the 3.0 license lets you do.

timesfm-non-commercial-license v1.0

You can: run it privately, experiment on any data, benchmark it, learn from it, and share what you found non-commercially. Personal research is exactly the intended use.

You can't: use the model or its forecasts for anything commercial or production — trading products, paid services, client work, business decisions — or redistribute the weights.

Need it for something commercial? Use TimesFM 2.5 (fully open, Apache-2.0 — the model in my chart above), or get a commercial license from Google.
