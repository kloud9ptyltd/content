03 · Your first backtest

Test a strategy in plain English.

Example: the classic "golden cross". Buy when the 50-day average crosses above the 200-day, sell when it crosses back below. Millions of traders use it. Does it actually work?

  • 1

    In the browser

    Open Strategy Backtesting → Strategy Builder. Pick the stock (e.g. AAPL), daily candles, and set the entry to "50-day average crosses above 200-day" and the exit to "crosses below". Run it.

    Strategy Backtesting ↗
  • 2

    Or ask the terminal's AI

    Open the assistant panel and type what you want, in normal words:

Type this into the assistantBacktest a golden cross on AAPL daily: buy when the 50-day moving average crosses above the 200-day, sell when it crosses below. Use all available history, include 0.1% commission per trade, and compare the result with simply buying and holding AAPL. Show me the equity curve and the trade list.

You get an equity curve, a trade list, drawdown, Sharpe ratio and win rate. Here's what we got when we ran exactly this on AAPL daily data, 2004 to 2026:

THE LESSON

A 70% win rate sounds amazing. But doing nothing made 14× more. That's why you test: what feels like an edge on a chart can lose to holding. Let the data prove your edge before your money does.

Our test used London Strategic Edge's bundled AAPL daily sample (split-adjusted), the Brue engine, 10-share entries and 0.1% commission per side. Past results don't predict future returns.

05 · Using it with Claude

Your own AI, same tools.

The terminal's assistant works out of the box with their own AI model, through your free key. If you already have Claude (or Codex or Gemini) installed on your computer, the terminal connects to it, and you use your own subscription.

Start in "Ask" mode. The AI asks before every change it makes. Avoid the "full" autonomy mode until you know what it's doing.
Be specific. Name the stock, the timeframe, the entry, the exit and the costs, like the example above.
Always ask it to compare with buy-and-hold. That one line is what keeps you honest.
06 · Good to know

Honest notes.

It's a test lab, not a trading platform. Charts refresh rather than stream every tick. Use it to research and backtest, then place real trades with your own broker.
Clean the data before you trust it. Some history includes odd prints. For example, AAPL briefly shows a $150 trade on 30 Oct 2026 in the overnight session. If a result looks too good, check the trades.
Free plan limits: 60 requests a minute, 50 GB of downloads a month and one live data connection at a time. Plenty for learning.
The desktop app is a beta and isn't Apple-notarised yet, so macOS may warn you when you open it. If you'd rather not, the browser version does most of the same things.
The 97% stat comes from a study of Brazilian day traders: of those who kept trading for 300+ days, about 97% lost money (summary ↗).
The $60: TradingView Premium, the plan with deep backtesting, is about $59.95 a month billed yearly (more if you pay monthly). Prices checked 2 Nov 2026.
It's a young company (founded 2025). Free plans can change, so grab what you need while it's free.
